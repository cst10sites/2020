The possibility of alien life

To fully understand the fermi paradox, we must first know what factors allow life to strive and develop, what conditions are suitable for the development of intelligent life, and how long it would take for intelligent life to develop and colonize its surrounding planets and possibly their entire galaxy.

Our universe is vast and endless, filled with billions of galaxies and billions of trillions of stars! Note: For the sake of simplification, all numbers used will be a rough estimation (numbers vary depending on sources).

There are billions of galaxies in the observable universe and in each galaxy there are hundreds billions of stars. It is estimated that there are (10 to the 22nd power) - (10 to the 24th power) stars out there. That means that for every grain of sand on every beach on earth, there are 10,000 stars out there. Of course, not all of those stars are suitable for the development of life. Depending on sources, 5 – 20% of stars are similar to our sun, which is around 500 quintillion stars. 22 – 50% of those sun-like stars have a planet that is similar to ours, in other words, a planet that can sustain life. That’s around 100 – 250 quintillion planets!

Now let’s assume that 1% of those planets develop life. That would still mean that there are 10 – 25 quadrillion intelligent species out there! Meaning that, in our galaxy alone, there are at least 100,000 intelligent civilizations!

Amazing right? But...if there are that many alien civilizations out there, and those civilization have had billions of years to colonize their galaxies, why haven’t we seen any signs of life yet?
