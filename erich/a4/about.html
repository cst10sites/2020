About

Overview

      The Oboe is a double reed, woodwind instrument. It is commonly found in symphony orchestras, concert bands, and ensembles. Someone who plays the Oboe is called an Oboist. There are several instruments in the Oboe family. Members of the Oboe family: heckelphone, bass oboe, cor anglais, oboe d'amore, oboe, piccolo oboe, and many more historical variants. The most common variants are the cor anglais, and the regular oboe, sometimes called the soprano oboe. This website will look at the soprano oboe, as it the most commonly known and widely used member of the family. Assume all further uses of the word Oboe refer to the Soprano variant unless otherwise specified.

Physical Overview

      The Oboe is skinny, long, and takes a conical shape through the body. It has a narrow inner bore with holes through to the keys. The outside takes resemblance of a Clarinet, however, the bell is much narrower, and the keys are more closed. The Oboe measures approximately 65cm long and varies in weight depending on the material. An Oboe is usually made in either wood or synthetic. It is made up of three main pieces: the top joint, middle joint, and the bell. The top and the middle have three main keys, with pinky keys on each. Oboes have between 16 and 24 keys which are all made of metal. On the top of the Oboe, there is a metal-sleeved hole to insert reeds. This is where the sound from the reed is passed through the instrument.


Reed-making tools. Top is a blank reed, a reed which has been tied, but not shaved. Middle is a reed-making knife, one of the most essential tools.

Sound & Reed

      Oboes are one of the few instruments which use double-reeds. A double-reed uses two pieces of cane tied together which vibrate to create a sound. The tips or reeds are thin and gradually thicken till the staple. The staple has a piece of cork where the reed fits into the slot on the top of an Oboe. Many advanced Oboe players make their reeds to suit their needs. The Oboe gets most of its sound from the reed, so it is important that players have reeds that work well with them. Oboe reeds are the smallest, but most important part of an Oboe.

      The Oboe's sound can be described as clear and penetrating. It is easily heard when playing and can be distinguished from other instruments. As mentioned above, it is a Soprano instrument and covers B♭3 to about G6. The first Oboist is the one who tunes the orchestra with a concert A.
