Basic Rules

Here is a list of 10 basic rules that all golfers should know. Recently, the USGA changed a couple of key rules, and I will include some if them in my list. It should be noted that there are 24 general rules of golf, each with 3-7 sub-rules.

10 Rules You Should Know

  1. You are allowed a maximum of fourteen golf clubs in your bag.
  2. In a competitive match, you are not allowed to give or receive advice from your opponent.
  3. When teeing off, you must hit from the parameters of the tee. Failure to do so will result in a two-stroke penalty.
  4. Make sure you are hitting your ball! If you accidentally hit someone else's ball, it's an automatic two-stroke penalty.
  5. Once the ball comes to rest on the putting surface you may mark it with a coin, or disk, lift and clean it but remember to replace in the exact spot.
  6. Previously, hitting the flagstick while putting from the surface of the green meant an automatic two-stroke penalty. Now, with the new changes, players are free to hit the flagstick from anywhere on the course penalty-free.
  7. Water hazards (now called penalty areas) are marked with red stakes, lateral water hazards are marked with yellow stakes, and out of bounds (OB) us marked with white stakes.
  8. Pace of play is very important in golf, especially in competitive golf. Most rounds should take four to four-and-a-half hours to complete, but a combination of bad players and poor course management can easily lead to a five, even six hour round. In recreational play, course marshalls may ask you to skip a hole in order to speed up your play, and the people playing behind probably won't be too happy. However, in competitive play, the organizaton governing the tournament has every right to deliver a two-stroke penalty or even disqualification if you continue to violate the pace of play rules.
  9. If your ball were to go into a hazard, out of bounds, or if you were to take an unplayable, previous rules stated that when dropping the ball, you had to do so from shoulder-length. However, now you are required to drop the ball from knee-height.
  10. And last but not least, always make sure to mark and sign your scorecard correctly! Handing in an incorrect scorecard can get you disqualified, or land you a heavy stroke penalty in the best-case scenario.
